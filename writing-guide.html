Practical Word Count and Writing Guide

Worked examples for essay word limits, character limits, editing and document word counts.

How to plan an essay word limit

Start by reading your assignment instructions: some institutions exclude references, appendices or tables, while others include them. The rules supplied with the task take priority over any general word-counting tool.

Worked example: an 800-word essay

Suppose your task is an 800-word discussion with an introduction, three body paragraphs and a conclusion. One possible draft budget is 100 words for the introduction, 200 words for each body paragraph, and 100 words for the conclusion. That adds up to 800 words. This is a planning example, not a requirement for every essay.

When editing, check whether each paragraph answers the question and supports its point with appropriate evidence. Avoid removing necessary citations just to meet a limit.

Worked example: cutting a 550-word draft to 500

First remove repeated ideas. Next, shorten long phrases without changing meaning. For example, “in order to explain” can become “to explain.” Finally, check whether the introduction or conclusion repeats the same information. Recount after each pass rather than deleting random sentences.

Word limits versus character limits

“Clear writing matters” has three words and 21 characters including the two spaces. A form asking for 100 characters is not asking for 100 words. Check the label on the form before editing your text.

What about punctuation and line breaks?

Our Word Counter counts words separated by whitespace. Other editors may count hyphenated expressions, symbols and abbreviations differently. Our Character Counter counts Unicode code points, so emoji made of several components may count differently on another platform.

Document upload checklist

  1. Check that the file is TXT, DOCX or a text-based PDF.
  2. Upload the file on the Word Counter page.
  3. Read the extracted text in the editor before relying on the count.
  4. Check tables, headers, footnotes and columns against the original document.
  5. For scanned PDFs, use an OCR application first; this website does not perform OCR.

Editing checklist

  1. Answer the question or satisfy the purpose of the text.
  2. Keep examples and evidence that support the main point.
  3. Remove repeated ideas and unnecessary filler.
  4. Review spelling, names, citations and formatting.
  5. Confirm the final count in the application where you will submit the text.

These examples are educational guidance, not a substitute for a university, employer or publisher's specific requirements.
