Practical Writing & Word Count Guide

Clear examples to help you plan, shorten and check your writing.

Whether you are writing an assignment, completing an application form or uploading a document, the goal is the same: understand what is counted and edit without losing important meaning.

1. Plan an essay before you start writing

A word limit is a space budget, not a target for adding filler. Before drafting, identify what your assignment asks you to explain, compare or argue. Then reserve most of the available words for the evidence and reasoning that answer the question.

Worked example: an 800-word discussion

Suppose you need an introduction, three body paragraphs and a conclusion. One possible allocation is:

SectionWordsPurpose
Introduction100Introduce the topic and main argument
Body paragraph 1200First point with evidence
Body paragraph 2200Second point with evidence
Body paragraph 3200Third point, comparison or limitation
Conclusion100Answer the question and synthesize
Total800Illustrative plan

Use this as a starting point, not a fixed formula. A laboratory report, reflective assignment and argumentative essay may require different structures. Always follow the marking rubric or submission instructions first.

What if the assignment allows a tolerance?

If an 800-word task explicitly permits 10% above or below the target, the numerical range is 720–880 words. Do not assume every assignment allows this: some impose a strict maximum, while others exclude references, captions or appendices.

2. Shorten a draft without removing the important ideas

The most effective cuts usually come from repetition and unnecessary phrases. Start with meaning, then improve individual sentences. Below are examples written for this guide.

Example A: remove unnecessary wording

Before: In order to make a decision about the next steps, the team held a meeting.

After: To decide the next steps, the team met.

The shorter version preserves the action while removing phrases that add little information.

Example B: remove repetition

Before: The survey results showed that students preferred online feedback. This preference for online feedback was clear in the survey responses.

After: The survey results showed that students preferred online feedback.

The second sentence repeats the first without adding evidence or explanation.

Example C: keep the qualification

Too strong: Online learning improves every student's performance.

More careful: Online learning may improve performance for some students, depending on course design and access to support.

Editing is not always about making a sentence shorter. Accurate qualifications matter more than saving a few words.

A practical editing order

  1. Highlight sentences that do not answer the question or support a point.
  2. Remove repeated explanations before deleting examples or evidence.
  3. Replace wordy phrases where the shorter form keeps the same meaning.
  4. Check that claims still have necessary citations and qualifications.
  5. Read the edited paragraph aloud to catch awkward transitions.
  6. Check the word count again in the application used for submission.

Try the changes in the Word Counter to compare the length of your original and revised drafts.

3. Understand word limits versus character limits

Words and characters are not interchangeable. A word count measures groups of text according to a counting rule; a character count measures letters, punctuation, spaces and sometimes line breaks. Different websites may count emoji differently.

Example: a form with a 150-character maximum

Write your response normally, then paste it into the Character Counter and set the limit to 150. The remaining-character display helps you see whether you are under or over the limit. Keep a small margin if the destination form has different rules for line breaks or emoji.

Important: Our Character Counter uses Unicode code points. A multi-part emoji may count as more than one character, while another platform may count it as one visible symbol or multiple code units.

4. Why two word counters can disagree

There is no single counting convention shared by every editor. SimpleToolsLab's Word Counter separates words at whitespace. This is useful for everyday text, but a university portal or word processor may apply different rules.

  • Hyphenated expressions: well-known is one whitespace-separated item here; another system may treat it differently.
  • Contractions: don't is one whitespace-separated item here, though tokenization rules can vary.
  • Numbers and symbols: A standalone symbol separated by spaces may be counted as an item by a simple whitespace-based tool.
  • References and captions: Some assignments exclude these from the permitted total; the tool does not automatically identify what your institution excludes.
  • PDF layouts: Columns, headers and tables can cause text to be extracted in an unexpected order.

For strict academic or professional submissions, follow the instructions of the destination system and use its final count as the deciding figure.

5. Check a PDF or Word document carefully

The Word Counter accepts TXT, DOCX and text-based PDF files. It extracts readable text into the editor so you can inspect the result before relying on the number.

  1. Open the Word Counter and choose your document.
  2. Wait for the processing message to finish.
  3. Review the extracted text, especially headings, tables, footnotes and columns.
  4. Compare the result with the original document if formatting matters.
  5. Confirm whether your assignment includes or excludes references, captions and appendices.

Scanned PDF example: If a PDF contains only photographs of printed pages, there may be no selectable text to extract. The Word Counter does not perform optical character recognition (OCR). Convert the scan to searchable text using a suitable OCR application before checking its count.

6. Frequently asked questions

Should I always write exactly the maximum number of words?

No. Aim to answer the question completely and clearly. A shorter, well-supported response is often preferable to a longer response filled with repetition, provided it meets any minimum length.

Should I count headings and citations?

Follow the specific instructions for your assignment or application. The tool counts text you paste or extract; it does not know which parts your assessor excludes.

Can I trust the count from an uploaded PDF?

Use it as a practical estimate and inspect the extracted text. Complex formatting and image-only pages can make PDF extraction incomplete.

How do I count text for a social profile or application form?

Use the Character Counter if the form specifies characters, and the Word Counter if it specifies words. Always check the destination form's own limit before submitting.

Does this guide replace my university's writing rules?

No. These are practical examples, not institution-specific requirements. Your assignment instructions, marking rubric and submission portal take priority.
